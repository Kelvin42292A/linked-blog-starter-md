## Material Facts

Nguyen was caught working on a remote, large-scale cannabis farm run by organized criminals. He claimed he initially thought he was planting "Italian tomatoes" and didn't know it was an illegal drug operation until later. When he found out and tried to leave, the bosses threatened to kill him and kidnap his son in Sydney if he escaped. The Crown argued he had worked there previously and must have known it was an illegal operation from the start, thereby voluntarily joining the criminal group.

## Issues

If the Crown alleges that an accused person voluntarily joined a criminal group, does the judge have the right to completely withdraw the defence of duress from the jury?

## Rule/Law

The defence of duress is **completely unavailable if the accused voluntarily put themselves in a position where they were likely to be subjected to illegal compulsion** (such as knowingly joining an illegal organization). However, if the accused claims they joined innocently and didn't know it was a criminal group, that is a factual question that only the **jury** is allowed to decide.

## Application/Analysis

Nguyen gave sworn evidence that he did not know he was being recruited for an illegal operation when he agreed to the farming work. Because he denied knowing it was a criminal enterprise, it was a question of fact for the jury to determine whether he should be disbelieved. The trial judge made a legal error by deciding this himself and hiding the defence of duress from the jury.

## Conclusion

The NSW Court of Criminal Appeal allowed Nguyen's appeal, quashed his conviction, and ordered a new trial so a jury could properly consider his duress claim.

### Judges Rules

Based on _Nguyen_ and the High Court principles discussed in _R v Lawrence_, the rule regarding voluntary involvement with criminal groups is:

1. **The "Prior Fault" Exception:** The defence of duress is completely stripped away if the accused, while free from threats, **knowingly and voluntarily exposes themselves to the risk of coercion**.
2. **Joining Criminal Groups:** If an accused knowingly joins an illegal organization, gang, or criminal enterprise, the law assumes they voluntarily put themselves in a position where they were likely to be subjected to illegal compulsion. They cannot later claim duress when that gang forces them to commit a crime.
3. **The Innocent Mistake Exception:** If the accused genuinely and innocently joined an enterprise without knowing its criminal or violent nature, they _can_ still raise the defence of duress when they are later threatened. Whether their involvement was truly "innocent" or "voluntary" is always a question for the jury to decide.
