**1. The Source of Executive Power**

- **Section 61:** The primary source of the Commonwealth's executive power is Section 61 of the Constitution.
- **Scope:** It authorizes the government to execute and maintain the Constitution and federal laws.
- **Prerogative Powers:** It encompasses traditional "Royal Prerogative" powers (like declaring war or entering treaties), adapted to suit Australia's status as an independent, sovereign nation.

**2. Parliament's Ability to Override Executive Power**

- **Subject to Laws:** The executive power is not absolute; it can be modified, regulated, or completely removed by laws passed by Parliament.
- **The "Clear Intention" Rule:** Parliament cannot accidentally remove an executive power. Displacing an executive power requires a **"clear and unambiguous provision"** or an "irresistible" implication in the statute.
- **Importance of Sovereignty:** The more significant an executive power is to Australia's national sovereignty, the harder it is to override. Courts require extremely clear and inescapable legislative wording to rule that a core sovereign power has been extinguished.

**3. Border Control and Excluding Non-Citizens**

- **National Sovereignty:** A central pillar of Australia's sovereignty is the power to determine who is allowed to enter its territory and who is admitted into the Australian community.
- **Inherent Power to Exclude:** Unless explicitly overridden by a specific statute, Section 61 gives the Executive government the inherent power to **prevent the entry of non-citizens** and to take the necessary steps to effect their exclusion.
