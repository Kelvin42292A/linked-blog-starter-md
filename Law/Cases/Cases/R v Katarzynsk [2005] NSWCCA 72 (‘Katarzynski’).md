## Material Facts

Katarzynski was heavily intoxicated at a hotel and got into a physical confrontation with Benjamin Hillier, who pushed/head-butted him. Katarzynski went outside into the car park. When Hillier approached him, Katarzynski drew a loaded revolver from his pants and pointed it at Hillier's waist to scare him. Hillier suddenly lunged forward with outstretched arms. Katarzynski claimed he "flinched" in response to the lunge, causing his finger to automatically and accidentally pull the trigger, firing three fatal shots. He argued his trial conviction for murder was invalid because the final trigger-pull was an involuntary "reflex action".

## Issues

If a person deliberately draws and points a loaded gun to scare someone, can they argue that a split-second "flinch" or "reflex" trigger-pull is legally an involuntary act that lets them escape liability for murder?

## Rule/Law

- **Spontaneous Reflex vs. Voluntariness:** A spontaneous, involuntary reflex is generally not a basis for criminal liability.
- **The Levelled Weapon Exception:** However, if a fully conscious person deliberately puts themselves in a dangerous situation by holding a loaded weapon with their finger on the trigger pointing at another person, a subsequent squeeze of the trigger in response to a sudden threat is **not legally involuntary**. Because the discharge of the weapon is a highly probable and foreseeable consequence of presenting a loaded gun, the entire sequence of events is treated as one **deliberate and voluntary composite act**.

## Application/Analysis

Katarzynski deliberately purchased a gun, loaded it, placed it in his pants, drew it, and pointed it to scare Hillier. Because he consciously put himself in this highly volatile position, his claim that he only fired because he "flinched" when Hillier lunged could not make the shooting involuntary. His physical actions, looked at as a whole, were legally voluntary.

## Conclusion

The NSW Court of Criminal Appeal dismissed his conviction appeal, upholding the voluntariness directions and his murder conviction

### Judges Rules

When a defendant claims they are not guilty of a violent crime because their physical reaction was an involuntary "reflex" or "flinch":

1. **Look at the Whole Sequence (The Composite Act):** Courts will not isolate a split-second muscle contraction (like squeezing a trigger) from the deliberate actions leading up to it.
2. **No "Reflex" Excuse for Self-Created Danger:** If a fully conscious person deliberately creates a highly dangerous situation (such as pointing a loaded firearm at someone), any subsequent automatic reaction to a sudden movement by the victim is **legally voluntary**.
3. **Foreseeability is Key:** A physical reaction is voluntary if it is a highly probable and foreseeable consequence of the defendant's own initial deliberate actions
