## Material Facts

- The Commonwealth entered into a funding agreement with Scripture Union Queensland (SUQ) to provide chaplaincy services at a state school under the National School Chaplaincy Program (NSCP).
- **Crucial detail:** This funding program was _not_ created under any legislation passed by Parliament; it was just an agreement administered directly by the Commonwealth.
- Ronald Williams, a parent of children at the school, challenged the validity of this agreement and the payments.

## Issues

- **Executive Power (s 61):** Did the Commonwealth have the executive power to enter this agreement and spend this money without an authorizing law?
- **Freedom of Religion (s 116):** Did requiring a "school chaplain" violate the constitutional rule against imposing a religious test for a public office under the Commonwealth?

## Rule & Application

- **Executive Power (s 61) – INVALID:** The High Court ruled by majority that the funding agreement and payments were **invalid** because they went beyond the Commonwealth's executive power. The Executive government cannot spend money or enter into contracts simply because Parliament _could_ theoretically pass a law allowing it; they need actual, existing statutory authorization, which was missing here.
- **Freedom of Religion (s 116) – DISMISSED:** The Court unanimously rejected the Section 116 challenge. Because the chaplains were hired by SUQ and did not have a contract with the government, they did not hold an "office under the Commonwealth," meaning the religious test prohibition did not apply.

## Conclusion

Mr. Williams won the primary constitutional argument. The High Court established a major universal rule: **The Commonwealth Executive does not have an unlimited, inherent power to spend money or enter contracts without explicit legislative approval from Parliament**.

### Judges Rules

- **No Unlimited Power to Spend:** The Commonwealth Executive government does not have the inherent power to spend money or enter into contracts simply because Parliament _could_ theoretically pass a law allowing it to do so.
- **Legislative Approval is Required:** In order to validly enter into funding agreements and distribute public money for programs (such as the school chaplaincy program), **the Executive must have actual, existing legislation passed by Parliament that explicitly authorizes the spending**. Section 61 of the Constitution alone is not enough to support this kind of spending without statutory authority.
