## Material Facts

Kurtic struck his neighbor with a tomahawk, causing serious head injuries. Charged with maliciously inflicting grievous bodily harm, Kurtic claimed self-defence, arguing he honestly believed his life was in danger. A psychiatrist testified that Kurtic _might_ have been experiencing paranoid delusions during the incident but conceded he had insufficient information to make a definitive judgment. The trial judge rejected Kurtic’s claims of delusions, found him to be the sole aggressor, and determined that the neighbor had offered no threat or physical provocation.

## Issues

1. **Delusional Self-Defence:** Can an accused rely on a personal characteristic (like a delusion) to claim they mistakenly perceived a threat if the victim did absolutely nothing in reality that could be mistaken as a danger?
2. **Role of Appeals:** Does the Court of Criminal Appeal (CCA) have the power to rehear the facts and make its own findings of guilt in an appeal from a judge-alone trial?

## Rule/Law

1. **Requirement of a Real-World Trigger:** For an accused’s personal characteristics (like a psychiatric delusion or intoxication) to be considered in self-defence, **there must be a reasonable possibility that at least some physical action took place in reality** that could have been mistaken as a threat or danger.
2. **Appellate Limits:** An appeal against conviction under the _Criminal Appeal Act 1912_ (NSW) is **not a rehearing**. The appellate court acts as a court of error and cannot rehear the trial issues or make its own factual findings.

## Application/Analysis

1. Because the neighbor did absolutely nothing that could be mistaken for a threat, there was no physical, real-world trigger for Kurtic's alleged delusion to act upon.
2. The trial judge's factual findings—that Kurtic was the aggressor and did not hold delusional beliefs at the time of the attack—were fully supported by the evidence and could not be overturned on appeal.

## Conclusion

The NSW Court of Criminal Appeal dismissed the appeal and upheld Kurtic's conviction.

### Judges Rules

- **Rule 1: The "Real-World Trigger" Rule for Delusional Self-Defence**  
    To argue that a mental condition or personal characteristic caused you to mistakenly perceive a threat, **some physical, objective event must have actually occurred in the real world** that you misconstrued. You cannot claim self-defence based on a threat that was entirely fabricated by a delusion with zero real-world trigger.
- **Rule 2: Appeals are Not Rehearings**  
    An appeal court cannot act as a "second trial" to rewrite history. Even in a judge-alone trial, the appellate court **cannot make its own fresh findings of fact** or rehear the witnesses; it can only intervene if the trial judge made a clear legal error or if there was no evidence to support their findings.
