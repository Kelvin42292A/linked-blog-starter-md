## Material Facts

Runjanjic and Kontinnen were severely abused by a domineering and violent man named Hill, whom they lived with. Under Hill's instructions and threats of violence, they lured another woman to their property, where Hill severely beat and falsely imprisoned her. The women claimed they acted under duress. At trial, the judge refused to allow a psychologist to testify about "battered woman syndrome" to explain why they did not escape, reasoning that the test for duress was purely objective.

## Issues

Can expert psychological evidence (like battered woman syndrome) be admitted to help a jury determine whether a person of "reasonable firmness" in the accused's situation would have reasonably believed they had to commit the crime?

## Rule/Law

The defence of duress involves both a subjective test (was the accused's will actually overborne?) and an objective test (would a person of reasonable firmness in their situation have been overborne?). Normally, juries decide what is "reasonable" without experts. However, because the situation of a habitually battered woman is so special and outside the ordinary experience of jurors, expert evidence is admissible to explain how a person of reasonable firmness _in that specific situation_ would perceive threats and their ability to escape.

## Application/Analysis

The trial judge was wrong to exclude the psychologist. The expert evidence was highly relevant because it helps the jury understand why a woman suffering from "learned helplessness" due to severe domestic violence might reasonably feel trapped, rather than just packing her bags and leaving like an average, un-battered person might expect.

## Conclusion

The Full Court of the Supreme Court of South Australia allowed the appeal, set aside the convictions, and ordered a new trial.

### Judges Rules (**Reasonable belief)**

When assessing what constitutes a **"reasonable belief"** or the reaction of a person of **"reasonable firmness"** in defences like duress or self-defence:

1. **The Contextual Objective Test:** The law does not judge the accused against an abstract, perfectly safe person. It asks what a reasonable person would do **in the specific situation of the accused**.
2. **Experts Can Explain the "Unreasonable":** If an accused's circumstances are completely outside ordinary human experience (such as enduring a cycle of habitual domestic violence), behaviors that seem "unreasonable" to a normal person—like failing to run away—can be explained by expert evidence.
3. **Battered Woman Syndrome:** Expert evidence of battered woman syndrome is legally admissible to show that a victim's heightened fear, sense of helplessness, and belief that they have no safe avenue of escape can actually be the expected, "reasonable" responses of someone trapped in that environment.
