## Material Facts

Viro was convicted of murder. He appealed on the grounds that the trial judge failed to properly instruct the jury on two issues: first, how his drug use (intoxication) affected his ability to form the intent to murder; and second, that they were allowed to find him guilty of manslaughter instead of murder if they found he acted in self-defence but used excessive force.

## Issues

1. If a defendant uses excessive force in self-defence, can they be convicted of manslaughter instead of murder?
2. How must a judge instruct a jury to consider an accused person's intoxication when deciding if they had the intent to commit a crime?
3. Is the High Court of Australia bound to follow the decisions of the Privy Council in the UK?

## Rule/Law

- **Excessive Self-Defence Rule:** If an accused uses more force than is reasonably proportionate to the threat they face, the verdict is **murder** if they _did not believe_ their force was proportionate. However, the verdict is **manslaughter** if they _did believe_ their force was proportionate, even if that belief was objectively unreasonable.
- **Intoxication Rule:** Where there is evidence of intoxication, the judge must tell the jury they can consider the intoxication to decide whether the accused actually formed the specific intent to commit the crime. If they are not satisfied the intent existed, they must acquit of that specific intent crime.
- **Precedent Rule:** The High Court of Australia is the ultimate court of appeal in Australia and is **not bound** by decisions of the Privy Council.

## Application/Analysis

The trial judge failed to instruct the jury that they could find Viro guilty of manslaughter if they believed he honestly thought his excessive force was necessary. The judge also failed to instruct them to consider his drug intoxication when determining if he actually formed the specific intent to murder.

## Conclusion

The High Court allowed the appeal (6:1) and set aside the murder conviction.

### Judges Rules

- **Rule 1: The "Honest Belief" Excessive Force Shield (Historical Rule)**  
    If you use excessive force in self-defence but **honestly believed** the amount of force you used was proportionate to the danger you faced, you cannot be convicted of murder—your liability is automatically reduced to **manslaughter**. _(Note: As discussed earlier, this rule was later abolished by Zecevic)._
- **Rule 2: Intoxication Can Negate Intent**  
    Evidence of intoxication cannot automatically buy a defendant an acquittal, but a jury **must be instructed to consider the intoxication** when deciding if the prosecution has proven that the accused actually formed the required specific intent for the crime.
- **Rule 3: Australian Judicial Independence**  
    The High Court of Australia is the final arbiter of Australian law and has the sole authority to decide what the law is, independent of British courts.
