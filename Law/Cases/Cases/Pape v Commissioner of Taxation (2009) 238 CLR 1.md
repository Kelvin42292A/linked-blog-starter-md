## Material Facts

- The Commonwealth Government passed the _(CTH) Tax Bonus for Working Australians Act (No 2) 2009_ to distribute tax bonus payments as a short-term fiscal stimulus to the national economy.
- The applicant, Pape, who was entitled to receive the bonus, sought a declaration that the Act was invalid and the payments were unlawful and void.
- The Commonwealth argued that the applicant only had standing to challenge his own payment, not the payments made to anyone else.

## Issues

1. **Standing:** Did the applicant have standing to challenge the validity of the entire Act?
2. **Validity of Appropriation & Executive Power:** Was the _Tax Bonus Act_ supported by a valid constitutional head of power (such as Executive Power under s 61) and a valid appropriation of funds under Sections 81 and 83?

## Rule/Law

- **Appropriation (ss 81 & 83):** Section 81 requires all revenues to go into the Consolidated Revenue Fund, and Section 83 dictates these moneys can only be appropriated for the "purposes of the Commonwealth".
- **Executive Power (s 61) & Incidental Power (s 51(xxxix)):** The Commonwealth's executive power extends to short-term fiscal measures designed to meet adverse economic conditions affecting the entire nation, provided they are within the government's capacity and resources.

## Application/Analysis

- **Standing:** The Court (led by French CJ) held that Pape did have standing because determining the validity of the entire Act was a necessary step in deciding the validity of the specific bonus payment he was entitled to receive.
- **Constitutional Validity:** The majority found that the need for an immediate fiscal stimulus to the national economy justified the Act. The legislation was validly supported by the combination of the Executive Power (s 61) and the Incidental Power (s 51(xxxix)), and it properly conformed to the appropriation requirements of ss 81 and 83.
- _(Dissent):_ Heydon J dissented, arguing the Act was invalid because the spending lacked a sufficient connection to trade and commerce, taxation, external affairs, or executive power.

## Conclusion

- The High Court determined that the _Tax Bonus for Working Australians Act (No 2) 2009_ was **constitutionally valid**. While the applicant successfully proved he had standing to bring the action, his challenge against the legality of the tax bonus payments failed.

### Judges Rules

- **Executive Power allows for National Crisis Management:** The Commonwealth Government's executive power (Section 61) gives it the authority to implement short-term financial measures (like a fiscal stimulus) to combat adverse economic conditions that affect the entire nation, provided the response is within the government's capacity and resources.
- **Spending must be for the Commonwealth:** Whenever the government appropriates funds for these measures, the money must strictly be used for the **"purposes of the Commonwealth"** under Sections 81 and 83.
