## **1. Timeline of Colonization (1770 – 1788)**

- **1770 – Captain Cook’s Voyage**: Captain James Cook sailed the _Endeavour_ [1]. On **April 29, 1770**, he raised the Union Jack at Botany Bay, claiming the eastern seaboard (naming it New South Wales) for the British Crown [1].
    - _Cook's Descriptions_: Reported fertile land but a "sparse" population with "no fixed habitation" (nomadic), observing they lived by fishing/hunting and never cultivated land [2].
- **The Dormant Period (1770 – 1788)**: British claims lay completely dormant for 18 years [2].
- **1783 – Renewed British Interest**:
    - The loss of the American colonies in the Revolutionary War ended transportation of British convicts there [2, 3].
    - Overcrowded prisons in Britain forced the use of **floating prison hulks** (decommissioned, unsanitary ships rife with cholera and high mortality rates) [2, 3].
    - New South Wales was identified as a viable solution for penal reform [3].
- **May 13, 1787**: The First Fleet departed Portsmouth under Captain Arthur Phillip, comprising 11 ships and over 1,500 people (including 736 convicts) [4].
- **January 1788**: Arrived at Botany Bay, which was deemed unsuitable [4, 5]. They moved north to **Sydney Cove (Port Jackson)** [4, 5].
- **February 7, 1788**: Formal proclamation of the colony of New South Wales [5].

## **2. The Doctrine of _Terra Nullius_**

- **Definition**: Latin translation for **"land belonging to no one"** [6].
- **Application**: Legally classified Australia as legally unoccupied or a "legal desert" [6, 7]. It was used to assert British sovereignty and completely ignore First Nations legal systems, sovereignty, and land ownership [1, 5, 6].
- **Three Methods of Acquiring Distant Lands (European International Law)**:
    1. **Conquest**: Taking land by force or war [7].
    2. **Cession**: Surrendering territory through treaties [7].
    3. **Settlement**: Occurred where land was uninhabited or "practically unoccupied" [7, 9].
- **The "Settled" Classification**: Because the British claimed the land was empty or inhabited by "uncivilized" peoples with no established laws, Australia was categorized as **settled** [6, 8, 9, 11]. Under this category, the colonizing power introduced British law wholesale in its entirety, acting as if nothing was there before [6, 8].
- **Legal Foundations**:
    - **William Blackstone's Principle**: Posited that settled territory vested in the Crown if it was uninhabited, or inhabited by people whose social organization was so "uncivilized" that English law could not recognize them [11].
    - **Doctrine of Discovery (15th Century)**: Originated in Papal Bulls, granting Christian monarchs the right to claim territories inhabited by non-Christians, deeming them "discovered" and open for European dominion [8].

## **3. Pre-Colonial Indigenous Reality (What Was Ignored)**

- **Continuous History**: Deep connection and stewardship of the land spanning over **65,000+ years** [12].
- **Complex Societies**: Over **250 distinct language groups** with highly unique governance and legal structures [15].
- **The Dreaming (Dreamtime)**: A fundamental, ongoing, and living spiritual worldview that encompasses creation, ancestral spirits, laws, customs, and deep reciprocal relationships with the land (which is viewed as a living entity) [12, 13, 19].
- **Sophisticated Land & Resource Management**:
    - _Fire Management_: Practicing "cool winter burns" to clear fuel loads and systematically prevent devastating hot summer bushfires [18].
    - _Agriculture_: Cultivating native grains (e.g., kangaroo grass) and yam gardens, challenging the colonial "hunter-gatherer" myth [19].
    - _Aquaculture_: Highly advanced Gunditjmara stone fish traps at Lake Condah (now a UNESCO World Heritage Site) [19].
    - _Lore & Justice_: Elders upheld restorative justice focused on reconciliation, compensation, and ceremonial harmony over punishment [15].
    - _Diplomacy/Trade_: Extensive networks trading resources like ochre from the Kimberleys across groups [15].
    - _Succession_: Traditional legal mechanisms where a neighboring group legally succeeds to the rights and interests of another group (often after a group was decimated) [16].

## **4. The Path to Native Title: _Mabo_**

### **Mabo v Queensland (No. 1) (1988)**

- **The Claim**: Eddie Mabo and other Murray Islanders asserted traditional ownership of the Murray Islands (Mer) in the Torres Strait [21, 22].
- **QLD Government Defense**: Passed the _Queensland Coast Islands Declaratory Act 1985_ to retrospectively extinguish all Native Title rights without compensation [22].
- **High Court Ruling**: Struck down the QLD law [23]. Because Europeans are compensated when their land is compulsorily acquired, denying compensation to Torres Strait Islanders was a direct breach of the Commonwealth’s _**Racial Discrimination Act 1975**_ [23, 32].

### **Mabo v Queensland (No. 2) (June 3, 1992)**

- **The Ruling**: Overturned _terra nullius_ once and for all [25].
- **Common Law Recognition**: Recognized "Native Title" as a pre-existing system of property rights under Australian common law, grounded in traditional law/custom and connection to land [25, 27].
- **Radical Title**: Ruled that upon settlement in 1788, the Crown did not acquire absolute _beneficial_ ownership of the land, but rather **radical title** [27]. Radical title means the Crown has the power to govern and make land grants, but must respect pre-existing native title unless it is validly extinguished [27].
- **Limitations**: _Mabo_ did not rule that colonization or Crown sovereignty was invalid [25].

## **5. Legislative Frameworks & Developments**

### **Native Title Act 1993 (NTA) — Commenced Jan 1, 1994**

- Codified the native title definition in **Section 223** [27].
- **Past Acts Validation**: Any Crown grants (mining, pastoral, freehold) made between 1975 (Racial Discrimination Act) and 1994 (NTA) that ignored native title were validated to prevent economic collapse [33, 34, 35]. The native title holders' lost rights were turned into compensation claims [33].
- **Future Acts**: Regulated any land dealings after Jan 1, 1994 [38]. To be valid under the RDA, developers/governments must follow non-discriminatory procedures (e.g., negotiating in good faith, conferring procedural rights) [38].
- **Indigenous Land Use Agreements (ILUAs)**: Formal, legally binding agreements made between developers/states and native title groups to gain **Free, Prior, and Informed Consent (FPIC)** for projects [39, 50, 51]. Sworn in via the National Native Title Tribunal (NNTT) [49].

### **The Wik Peoples v Queensland (1996)**

- **The Question**: Did standard pastoral leases extinguish native title [36]?
- **The Ruling**: High Court found that pastoral leases (even massive ones) do not automatically grant exclusive possession and therefore **do not extinguish native title**; the two can legally coexist [28, 37, 56].
- **The Legislative Response (1998)**: John Howard's government passed the "Ten-Point Plan" amendments, validating "intermediate period acts" (grants made between 1994 and 1996) and creating a schedules list of acts that extinguish native title [37, 56, 57].

## **6. Compensation & "Cultural Loss"**

The landmark **Timber Creek (2019)** case established the legal methodology for assessing Native Title compensation on a bifurcated basis [74]:

1. **Economic Loss**: Assessed by calculating what the market value of the land would be if it were freehold title [58].
2. **Cultural Loss**: A non-economic compensation head for the pain, suffering, hurt, and spiritual damage caused by breaking the community's connection to country and interfering with sacred Dreaming stories [14, 59, 74].
    - _Escalation_: Compensation sums have grown rapidly—from $3 million in _Timber Creek_ [85] to $150 million awarded in the **_Yindjibarndi_ (2020/21)** Fortescue metals dispute [24, 85].

## **7. Protection Regimes, Stolen Generations, and Walk-ins**

- **Protection Regimes**: Nineteenth and twentieth-century policies placed Indigenous Australians under "protectors" as wards of the state [68, 69]. Protectors controlled their movement, labor, and directly withheld their wages (resulting in **"Stolen Wages" class actions** settled for hundreds of millions in QLD/NT) [69].
- **Stolen Generations**: Forcible removal of Indigenous children from their families to suppress language and culture [68].
    - _Apology_: Kevin Rudd delivered the federal apology in **2008** (without offering a direct compensation scheme) [69].
- **Criminal Law & Sovereignty**: In **Walker v Queensland (1994)**, the High Court held that general criminal law applies uniformly to all people [64]. Customary criminal law was extinguished by general criminal statutes of application [64].
- **Indigenous Sentencing Courts**: Introduce elders into lower magistrates courts to provide cultural advice on sentencing, but do not replace state laws [70].

## **8. Constitutional & Representative Milestones**

- **1967 Referendum**: Passed with an overwhelming **90%+ majority** [71].
    - Amended **Section 51(xxvi)** (the race power) to allow the Commonwealth to make laws for Indigenous Australians (previously restricted to states) [71].
    - Repealed **Section 127** to ensure Indigenous Australians were counted in the national census [71].
    - _Note_: Compulsory voting was not fully extended to Indigenous Australians until 1984 [72].
- **2023 Voice Referendum**: A proposal resulting from the **Uluru Statement from the Heart (2017)** [75] seeking to establish a constitutionally enshrined advisory body ("The Voice to Parliament") [76].
    - _Result_: Failed to pass, securing only 40% Yes [77]. It failed the double majority requirement [75] due to concerns over legal uncertainty, claims of division, and widespread misinformation [77].
