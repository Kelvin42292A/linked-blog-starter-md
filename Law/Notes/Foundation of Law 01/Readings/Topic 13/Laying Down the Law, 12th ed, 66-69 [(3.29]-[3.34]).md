**1. State & Territory Courts**

- **Separation of Powers:** Unlike the federal government, state and territory courts do not have a constitutionally entrenched separation of powers.

**2. The Executive Branch**

- **What it does:** It administers and enforces the law.
- **Key Players:** It includes the Governor-General, Prime Minister, Cabinet Ministers, and government departments.
- **Constitutional Quirk:** Surprisingly, the _Australian Constitution_ does not actually mention the Prime Minister or the Cabinet.
- **How it works:** The Governor-General has limited independent "reserve powers," but mostly acts on the advice of Ministers who hold the real power.

**3. Keeping the Government in Check (Administrative Law)**

- **Administrative Law:** Sets legal limits on government power so citizens can challenge unfair actions.
- **Merits Review:** Special tribunals can step in and completely remake a government decision.
- **Judicial Review:** Courts can check a government action, but only to see if it was strictly legal.
- **Watchdogs:** Bodies like the Ombudsman, Auditor-General, and Royal Commissions investigate complaints and force the government to fix its mistakes.

**4. International Law**

- **Public vs. Private:** Public international law deals with rules between sovereign nations, while private international law deals with individuals or businesses crossing borders.
- **Where it comes from:** It is created through general, accepted customs (customary law) or written agreements between countries, such as treaties and conventions.
