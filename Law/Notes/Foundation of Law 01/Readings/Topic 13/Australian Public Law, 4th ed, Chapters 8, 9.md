**🏛️ The Australian Government & Accountability**

**1. Parliaments (The Lawmakers)**

- Australia has federal, state, and territory parliaments that make our laws.
- Their power is not absolute; it is limited by the Constitution and special voting rules (called **"manner and form" provisions**) that make it harder to change certain important laws.
- Members of Parliament have **"parliamentary privilege"**, which gives them legal immunities like freedom of speech so they can debate without fear of being sued.

**2. The Executive (The Administrators)**

- This branch includes the **Governor-General, Prime Minister, Cabinet, and government departments**.
- Their job is to put laws into action and run the country.
- Their powers come from three main places: the Constitution, specific laws passed by parliament (statutory powers), and historical powers inherited from the British Crown (**prerogative powers**).

**3. Keeping the Government in Check** Because the Executive holds a lot of power, there are systems in place to hold them accountable:

- **Parliamentary Scrutiny:** Parliament keeps the government honest through **"Question Time"** and detailed investigations by **parliamentary committees**.
- **Administrative Law:** This area of law allows everyday citizens to challenge unfair government decisions.
    - **Merits Review:** Special tribunals (like the Administrative Appeals Tribunal) can step in, look at the facts, and completely remake a government decision.
    - **Judicial Review:** Courts can look at a government decision, but only to check if it was made strictly according to the law.
- **Watchdogs & Transparency:**
    - **The Ombudsman** investigates complaints from the public about government agencies.
    - **Royal Commissions** are the highest form of inquiry used to investigate massive systemic failures or corruption.
    - **Freedom of Information (FOI) laws** allow citizens and journalists to request and access official government documents.
